# BDN Style Rules

This file is the rule source for the `bdn-style` skill.

## Rule Priority

Apply rules in this order:

1. User request and assignment-specific instructions
2. The canonical rules in this file
3. General AP-style conventions as fallback when this file is silent

## Voice and Tone

- Write in plain, direct English.
- Prefer specific language over vague framing.
- Keep copy disciplined and non-promotional.
- Avoid inflated adjectives unless directly supported by reporting.
- Preserve regional context when relevant to Maine readers.

## Canonical Rules

Use the entries below as authoritative defaults.

### General

- `800-THI-SWAY`: Use area codes in all phone numbers.
- `Accent marks`: Do not use accent marks, umlauts, or other special characters.
- `Acres`: Do not write "acres of land"; use constructions like "an 80-acre parcel" or "80 forested acres."
- `Acronyms`: Do not use abbreviations or acronyms that are not universally recognizable; prefer a short noun on second reference.
- `Addiction`: Do not use "addicts" or "users"; prefer "addiction" or "alcoholism" over "substance use disorder"; avoid "clean" or generic "harm reduction".
- `Advocacy websites`: Citing advocacy sites is acceptable if motives are transparent; avoid calling them "news" sites.
- `Anniversary`: Use "first anniversary" or "10th anniversary," not "one-year anniversary."
- `Annual`: Always lowercase; never use "first annual."
- `Anti-abortion`: Use "anti-abortion," not "pro-life"; do not describe a fetus as an "unborn child" in news reporting.
- `At this time`: Avoid this phrase.
- `Attribution`: Use past tense ("said"); use subject-verb order ("subject said"); keep "said" close to quote; do not start sentences with attribution.
- `-bound`: Avoid redundant phrasing like "traveling eastbound"; use "eastbound."
- `Brackets`: Use brackets `[]`, not parentheses `()`, when adding context to a quote.
- `Canadian communities`: Refer to town and province (for example, "Saint John, New Brunswick"); do not add "Canada."
- `Capital/capitol`: Capital is a city; capitol is a building; in Maine use "State House" for where the Legislature meets.
- `Capitalization`: Do not use all-caps for business names (for example, "Lego," not "LEGO").
- `Cliches`: Avoid cliches.
- `Comparing things`: Use "compared with" for similarities and differences; use "compared to" to assert similarity.
- `Could possibly`: Avoid; "could" already implies possibility.
- `Current`: Use only when needed for clarity.
- `Data`: Treat as singular ("The data shows...").
- `Date ranges`: Use "from 1998 to 2019" or "1998-2019"; never "from 1998-2019."
- `Day of the week`: Include day of week with date; use "today" and "tomorrow" when applicable, especially in headlines.
- `Dunkin'`: Always use "Dunkin'."
- `Electrocution`: Use only if death occurred; otherwise use "electric shock."
- `Exclusivity`: Use "told the Bangor Daily News" once; for documents use "obtained by the Bangor Daily News."
- `Flyer`: Use "flyer" for people who fly and advertisements.
- `Fly tier`: Use "fly tier" for fishing; never "fly tyer."
- `Freshman`: Appropriate for sports and politics; for students, use year of study or age.
- `Gender`: Ask for pronouns; mention transgender status only if relevant; use "assigned male/female at birth" rather than "biologically male/female."
- `General Assistance`: Capitalize as specific government aid program.
- `Greater (city)`: Use "Greater Bangor," not "the Greater Bangor area."
- `Harbor master`: Two words.
- `Homeless`: Acceptable adjective; avoid "the homeless"; mention only if relevant; avoid disparaging terms.
- `Hunters breakfast`: Use "hunters breakfast," not hunters' or hunter's breakfast.
- `Hyphens`: Do not use hyphen to connect an adverb to another word; use for compound modifiers only when needed for clarity. Do not remove hyphens that are part of proper names (e.g., Can-Am, T-Mobile).
- `Impact`: Never use as a verb.
- `Increase/decrease`: Prefer descriptive verbs like "spike," "boost," "drop," or "plummet" when accurate.
- `Indigenous/Native American`: Capitalize Indigenous; use tribal affiliation for specific people; "Wabanaki tribes" is the collective term in Maine.
- `Individual/Individuals`: Use "person" or "people" instead.
- `LGBTQ+`: Preferred acronym; use only when relevant.
- `Mill rate`: Not "mil rate."
- `Millworker`: One word.
- `Obscenities`: Avoid unless absolutely necessary and approved by a senior editor.
- `Offices`: Use lowercase for offices (for example, "state fire marshal's office").
- `Omit needless words`: Use as few words as possible; avoid "both," "that," and "in order to" where unnecessary.
- `Opiates vs. opioids`: Opiates are poppy-derived; opioids include synthetic products.
- `Percent`: Use `%` with numbers; spell out "zero percent."
- `Percentage points`: Distinguish percent change from percentage-point difference.
- `People-first language`: Put person before disability/identity where applicable.
- `Queer`: Use only if the subject identifies as such.
- `Quoting`: Quotes must add value; avoid block quotes; use brackets for clarity.
- `Racially charged`: Avoid; describe behavior directly; "racist" may be used when apt.
- `Reform`: Avoid repetitive use; prefer "policy changes" where that is more precise.
- `Senior`: Avoid "elderly" and "senior citizen"; use age instead.
- `Semicolons`: Avoid unless separating items in a list; prefer period.
- `Sexual orientation`: Use "gay" or "lesbian"; include only if pertinent; avoid "lifestyle" or "preference."
- `Signs`: Not "signage."
- `Spokesperson`: Use instead of spokesman/spokeswoman.
- `Subject`: Do not call a person "a subject"; use specific role such as witness or suspect.
- `Suicide`: Follow "Reporting on Suicide" best practices.
- `Temperatures`: Use "lower" or "higher," not "colder" or "warmer."
- `Time Day Date`: Format as `time day, date, place` with no comma after time (for example, "5 p.m. Saturday, July 31").
- `Titles (personal)`: Capitalize only before a name.
- `Titles (things)`: Use quotes for books, songs, albums, and movies; no quotes for reference books or most symphonies.
- `Toward`: No "s" in U.S. usage.
- `Transported`: Use "taken" instead.
- `Vessels`: Use "the future USS Whatever" for ships under construction; no periods in USS.
- `Victim/survivor`: Use with care; be specific about injuries/ordeals; reflect language used by subject.
- `Viral`: Use judiciously; "widely shared" is often sufficient.
- `Walmart`: One word, no hyphen.
- `Websites and URLs`: Link to websites; keep URLs short.

### Maine

- `Antlerless deer permits`: Use "antlerless deer permits," not "deer permits."
- `Bangor Daily News`: Use full name on first reference; `BDN` subsequently.
- `The County`: Aroostook County is "The County" — capitalize both words, including "The," on every reference, even mid-sentence.
- `Down East`: Two words, uppercase; refers to Washington County.
- `Katahdin`: Use "Katahdin," not "Mount Katahdin."
- `MaineCare`: Maine's version of Medicaid.
- `Mainer`: Preferred over "Maine resident."
- `Midcoast`: One word, lowercase, no hyphen.
- `North Woods`: Two words, capitalized. This refers to the generic regional term only. "North Maine Woods" is a distinct proper name — both a geographic region and the name of a nonprofit land management organization. Do not change "North Maine Woods" to "North Woods."
- `Roads`: Do not use "the" before road names except for "the Golden Road."

### Police and courts

- `Attorney vs. lawyer`: Use "attorney" for trial representation; "lawyer" as job description.
- `Car crashes`: Use "crashes," not "accidents"; use "suffered" for injuries, not "sustained."
- `Charged/accused/alleged`: Specify the charge; use "suspect" until convicted; avoid double attribution.
- `Courts`: Uppercase District Court, Superior Court, and U.S. Supreme Court; lowercase "supreme court" for Maine's high court.
- `Murder`: Use "homicide" or "slaying" until conviction for murder.
- `Pleaded`: Use "pleaded," never "pled."

### Politics

- `Legislative bodies`: Uppercase specific community bodies; lowercase general references.
- `Represent!`: Use formats such as "Sen. Debra Plowman, R-Hampden" or "U.S. Sen. Susan Collins, R-Maine."

## QA Checklist

Before returning output, confirm:

- The lead reflects the strongest verified fact.
- No facts were added or altered.
- Attribution is clear for contested or non-obvious claims.
- Canonical term choices above are followed for relevant topics.
- Grammar and punctuation are publication-ready.
